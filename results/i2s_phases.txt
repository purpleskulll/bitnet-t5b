==================================================================
 WHERE llama.cpp's i2_s MATMUL TIME GOES
==================================================================

  Reproduce: build with -DBITNET_I2S_PHASES (scripts/build_insitu.sh
             then re-configure with that flag), run llama-bench on the
             i2_s model, read the counters from the [bitnet-t5b] line.

  §7.4 measures llama.cpp's i2_s path at 1.257x the cost of t5b's and
  names three candidates for where that goes -- dispatch, the accumulator
  fold, and the per-column post-processing -- without separating them,
  because one probe at one site yields one number. Two more inside
  tinyBLAS_I2S_AVX do separate them: one around the contraction loop, one
  around the post-processing. DISPATCH is the remainder, measured by
  difference, because a probe around the call would be the call.

  THE PROBES ARE OFF BY DEFAULT AND THAT IS NOT TIDINESS. They fire once
  per RM x RN tile, far more often than the dispatch-site probe, and they
  instrument ONE arm -- so leaving them in inflates the i2_s side of the
  ratio. Measured on this host: 1.379 with them against 1.257 without, a
  10 % artefact entirely of the instrument's making. The decomposition
  and the ratio therefore come from two different builds, by design.

  run       dispatch-site      contraction        post-proc
  1           11144914200      10526066073        344706085
  2           11638921680      10956453989        385562197
  3           11833963848      11178760661        369690157
  4           11193180408      10590615522        334934355
  5           10942136208      10333490010        330082200

  mean over 5 runs:
    contraction       94.4 %
    post-processing    3.1 %
    dispatch (rest)    2.5 %

  READ: the cost is the contraction loop itself. Neither the dispatch nor
  the per-column post-processing is where llama.cpp's i2_s path spends
  its time, so the 1.257 reflects the inner loop's own arithmetic and
  memory traffic rather than overhead around it. Two of §7.4's three
  candidates are ruled out by these numbers. The third -- the accumulator
  fold -- is answered by READING the kernel instead: tinyBLAS_I2S_AVX has no
  32-block fold at all. Its _mm256_madd_epi16 sits inside the block loop, so
  it folds int16 into int32 once per 128-weight block, where the vec_dot
  reference accumulates 32 blocks first (group32_num = nb / 32). The fold is
  therefore not a periodic interruption of the contraction; it is part of
  every block, and it is inside the 94.4 %.

  A CONSEQUENCE FOR SECTION 7.1. The int16 overflow measured there --
  11,998 of 12,000 rows at K = 6912 -- is a property of the REFERENCE kernel,
  which has the 32-block group. The path a real run dispatches does not have
  it and cannot overflow that way. The paper said "llama.cpp's i2_s AVX2
  kernel" without distinguishing the two, which reads as the stronger claim;
  it now names the reference.
