==================================================================
 THE SHIPPING i2_s KERNEL FOLDS TEN TIMES MORE OFTEN THAN IT MUST
==================================================================

  Reproduce: scripts/build_insitu.sh, then a second tree configured with
             -DBITNET_FOLD10, and llama-bench on the i2_s model. The
             correctness check is llama-perplexity on the same corpus.

  THE BOUND, computed from the kernel's own operands. tinyBLAS_I2S_AVX feeds
  vpmaddubsw with RAW 2-bit codes (0..3, unsigned) against int8 activations, so
  one int16 lane holds at most 2 * 3 * 127 = 762 per vpmaddubsw and at most
  4 * 762 = 3048 per 128-weight block. The int16 ceiling is 32767, so

      floor(32767 / 3048) = 10 blocks may be accumulated before folding.

  WHAT THE TWO KERNELS ACTUALLY DO

      bitnet_vec_dot_i2_i8_s_reference   folds every 32 blocks
      tinyBLAS_I2S_AVX (what dispatches) folds every  1 block
      ternary_t5b_dot_avx2               folds every 12 blocks (its own bound
                                         is 12 * 2560 = 30720 <= 32767)

  The reference kernel's 32 is ABOVE its own safe limit of 10, and that is the
  whole of the overflow section 7.1 measures: 32 * 3048 = 97536, three times
  the ceiling. The shipping kernel's 1 is a factor of ten BELOW the limit, and
  costs accordingly.

  WHAT THE NINE OMITTED FOLDS COST. A tree identical but for accumulating ten
  blocks in int16 before folding, three runs each, interleaved:

      folding every block   10,254,922,296 cycles   (mean of 3)
      folding every ten      9,488,166,828 cycles   (mean of 3)
      difference                      7.5 %

  Perplexity is IDENTICAL between the two builds -- 108.6810 +/- 13.55672 on
  four chunks of WikiText-2 -- which is what makes this a cost and not a
  trade-off.

  WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SAY

  It accounts for 7.5 of the roughly 25 % by which llama.cpp's dispatched i2_s
  path is slower than t5b's (the measured ratio is 1.257). It does NOT account
  for the rest, and it is not the explanation of that ratio -- it is one
  component of it, measured rather than argued.

  It is also NOT A PROPOSAL. The i2_s path is the control arm of every A/B this
  project reports, and a result measured against a moved baseline is not a
  result. The modified kernel lives in its own tree, is never built into the
  binary any reported ratio uses, and exists to answer one question: how much
  of the shipping path's cost is a fold interval that its own operand bound
  does not require.

  A reader who wants the upstream kernel faster has the answer here; this work
  does not take it.

  AND THE SAME ARITHMETIC WAS TURNED ON t5b, which is the point of computing a
  bound rather than quoting one. Its digits are 0..2 against int8, so an int16
  lane takes at most 2 * 2 * 127 = 508 per vpmaddubsw and 5 * 508 = 2540 per
  160-weight block:

      floor(32767 / 2540) = 12 blocks, and TERNARY_T5B_FOLD is 12.

  It sits exactly at its limit, not below it, so there is no equivalent saving
  to take. The header derives the same 12 from a slightly conservative 2560 per
  block; both give 12, and the tighter figure changes nothing.

  Two things were checked rather than assumed. The bound does NOT depend on K:
  it is per block, and a lane never sees more than TERNARY_T5B_FOLD blocks
  however long the row -- K = 6912 is 43 full blocks, folded in groups of 12.
  And all THREE t5b paths use the same interval, the GEMM one per column
  (ternary_t5b.c:289, :411, :719), so the blocking of columns does not weaken
  it.
