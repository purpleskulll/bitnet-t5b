==============================================================================
 DELETING THE FOURTH MAGIC MULTIPLY: the measurement
 2026-09-13, Ryzen 5 3600 (Zen 2, AVX2+FMA, no VNNI), quiet box
==============================================================================

WHAT IS BEING MEASURED

q3 = floor(x/27) is at most 9 for every byte, so it is a NIBBLE, and vpshufb can
index it. Three constant 16-entry tables then give, from q3 alone:

    d4 = floor(q3/3)      (= floor(x/81), by the nested-floor identity)
    d3 = q3 mod 3
    3*q3                   (for d2 = q2 - 3*q3)

The /81 multiplier 811 and its two vpmulhuw disappear, and with them the whole
q4 register. The file format does not change: same five base-3 digits per byte,
same 1.60755 bits/weight, same packed bytes, so no repack and no compatibility
break. The variant is behind -DTERNARY_T5B_SHUFDIG.

This came out of a research wave as its one surviving proposal. The wave's own
refuter measured it and reported numbers; everything below was measured here,
independently, with a harness written here rather than with the one it left.

THE BASELINE ARM IS PROVED TO BE THE SHIPPED KERNEL, NOT ASSUMED TO BE

The prototype file carries both arms behind #ifdef. Compiled without the -D it
emits an inner loop BYTE-IDENTICAL to src_modifications/ternary_t5b.c's. So the
comparison is today's kernel against itself plus one change, and not against a
five-day-old copy of itself.

    gcc -O3 -mavx2 -mfma -march=native -std=c11     (the Makefile's own CFLAGS)

                 instructions   vector ops   multiply-port   vpmulhuw  vpshufb
    baseline          46            43            13             8         0
    SHUFDIG           40            37            11             6         3

43 is the figure ternary_t5b.c's header records, so this reproduces it exactly.
NOTE THE FLAGS: without -march=native the same source gives 42 and 36. An
op-count acceptance criterion without its flags is not a criterion --
scripts/microarch_model.sh omits -march=native and the Makefile sets it, so the
two disagree by two ops on identical source.

CORRECTNESS, at the real artefact and not at a model of it

    src/test_t5b.c linked against the SHUFDIG object:
        97,529 checks, 0 failures

That includes 73,277 column-blocked GEMM cells compared against scalar, 3,523
row-tiled GEMV rows, the fold-boundary cases, and the all-zero row identity.
Separately, an exhaustive comparison over all 256 byte values (including the
thirteen foreign ones above 242) shows the two decoders produce identical digit
planes, and that both agree with the true base-3 digits on x <= 242.

ROUND 4 IS MINE AND IS LEFT IN. While the run was going I started a second
benchmark on the same pinned core, and it stole the core during the shuf arm's
t5b window -- the i2_s figure from the SAME process is 79.03, i.e. normal, so
the contention was local to that window. It is recorded rather than deleted
because it is the only honest demonstration of why this design takes a median:
one round at 0.61 moves the median by nothing.

THE CONTROL IS THE POINT. The i2_s kernel is byte-identical in the two binaries
and still ranges 0.988 to 1.039 round to round. That 5% band IS this
measurement's noise floor, and 1.2254 sits far outside it.

THE WIDE PATH GAINS NOTHING, AND THE REASON IS MEASURABLE

No benchmark in this repository drives ternary_t5b_gemm_avx2 in isolation, so
the wide path had never been measured here at all. The refuting agent reported
0.9965x at NC=8 -- prompt processing gains NOTHING -- which is the single
most consequential thing it said, because it decides whether the improvement may
be quoted next to pp512. benchmarks were written for this and swept the strip
width. Nine interleaved rounds, n = 6912, the model's hidden dimension:

      cols      base med     shuf med     ratio    shuf faster
        1         34.95        40.57     1.1608        9 of 9
        4         66.71        75.80     1.1363        9 of 9
        8         78.04        77.55     0.9937        3 of 9
       16         78.08        78.19     1.0014        4 of 9
       32         78.11        77.87     0.9969        4 of 9
       64         77.88        77.54     0.9956        5 of 9

The agent was right and its number reproduces: 0.9937 here against its 0.9965.

THE SHAPE IS THE FINDING, and it was not in the agent's report. The GEMM decodes
each 32-byte block ONCE and spreads it across `cols` activation columns, so the
decode is amortised over the strip width. By cols = 8 BOTH arms saturate at the
same ~78 GMAC/s, and from there on 3 to 5 wins out of 9 is a coin flip. That
saturation at a common rate is the signature of a shared bottleneck: past width
8 the cost is the contraction and the activation stream, and the decode -- the
only thing this change touches -- has been amortised away entirely.

So the improvement is confined to narrow strips by construction:

    token generation, batch 1, one column   -> gains
    prompt processing, wide strips          -> gains nothing, and cannot

Any write-up that puts a speedup next to pp512 is wrong. tg128 is the only
headline number this can move.

IT IS FASTER THAN THE PAPER'S OWN MODEL PREDICTS, AND THE MIX SAYS WHY

ternary_t5b.c's header states that the loop is bound by the TOTAL vector op
count and that "the only thing that moves it is removing ops". Taken literally
that predicts 43/37 = 1.1622. The multiply-port model predicts 13/11 = 1.1818.
The measurement is 1.2254, above both.

The instruction mix of the two loops, counted in the emitted code:

                                  baseline   SHUFDIG
      vpmulhuw                        8          6
      vpmaddubsw                      5          5
        multiply port, total         13         11
      vpsllw / vpsrlw                 9          6
      vpshufb                         0          3
        shift+shuffle pair, total     9          9
      vpsubb                          4          3
      vpor                            4          3
      vpaddw                          9          7
      vpand, vmovdqu                  3          3

The multiply port falls from 13 to 11 while the shift-and-shuffle pair stays at
exactly 9: three shifts are replaced by three shuffles, which is the trade the
proposal makes, and it is neutral on that pair BY CONSTRUCTION. So the
distribution across ports flattens.

Backing the rates out: the baseline sustains 43 ops / 19.7 cycles = 2.18 vector
ops per cycle. The kernel header states the same quotient as 2.19 --
src_modifications/ternary_t5b.c:212, "43 / 19.7 = 2.19 vector ops per cycle" --
so this is not the figure the header records, it is the same division rounded
the other way: 43/19.7 is 2.1827. Nothing here turns on the third digit; both
readings are the ~2 vector ops per cycle that a Zen 2 part can issue, which is
the point the header is making. At 1.2254x the block takes 16.08 cycles, so
SHUFDIG sustains 37 / 16.08 = 2.30. The remaining ops issue BETTER, not merely
fewer.

STATED AS WHAT IT IS: the flatter port distribution is CONSISTENT with the extra
5%, and it is not isolated by this measurement. Two candidate mechanisms --
better issue from a flatter mix, and a shorter dependency chain on two of the
five digit planes -- are not separated here, and neither is register pressure,
though that one can be ruled out: both loops spill ZERO times.

What is established is narrower and still worth having: the header's model is
incomplete. Removing ops is not the only lever; which port they came off
matters too.

WHAT IT DOES TO S, AND TO THE PROFITABILITY CONDITION

    measured here today:  S = 78.30 / 32.85 = 2.384   (canonical 2.4283; this
                          box runs ~2% friendlier than the recorded figures)
    with the shuffle:     S = 2.384 / 1.2254 = 1.945
    applied to the canonical pair: 2.4283 / 1.2254 = 1.982

The condition is S < Sigma, with Sigma measured at 1.35 / 1.63 / 1.78 / 1.86 at
one to four threads and 3.03 at six.

    NOT ONE CELL FLIPS. At six threads it paid before and pays now. At one to
    four threads it failed before and fails now.

What changes is the margin, and only at the top of that range: the four-thread
cell goes from 31% short (2.4283 against 1.86) to 6.5% short (1.982 against
1.86). The remaining gap is smaller than the improvement just made.

This is the honest framing and it should be used: the change helps exactly where
the format was LOSING and does nothing where it was already winning, because at
six threads t5b already sits at the memory knee and more arithmetic cannot be
spent.

END TO END, as a bound rather than a claim

The in-situ counters put the matmul at 43.6% of a generated token and the
contraction at 94.4% of the matmul, so 41.2% of the token. Applying the full
1.2254 to that fraction gives tg128 25.78 -> 27.9 t/s, +8.2%.

THAT IS AN UPPER BOUND AND NOT A PREDICTION. The 1.2254 is an arithmetic rate
with memory taken out; in situ the matmul is partly memory-bound, so the
realised share is smaller. Whoever quotes an end-to-end number must measure it
in situ rather than derive it here.
